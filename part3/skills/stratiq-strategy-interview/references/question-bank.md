# Strategy Interview: question bank

Pick follow-ups from here when an answer is vague, one-sided or untested. Each points to the evidence
it relies on. Never use them to steer toward a particular strategy.

## Opportunity (strategic maps, whitespace, trends)

- Which competitors share a strategic group with us today? What happens to profit inside that group?
- Is the empty space empty because nobody has thought of it, or because nobody wants it? What evidence
  says customers would pay for it? (Blue-ocean candidates still `demand: unpriced` are untested.)
- Which trending influence factor did you rely on? What would it take for that trend to stall?
- If two of us pick different spaces, what does each of us believe about the market that the other
  doesn't?
- Which segment is growing fastest, and is anyone serving it well?

## Advantage (KSFs, VRIO, value chain)

- On the KSF that matters most in that space, where do we rank? What would move us up one place?
- Which of our advantages is rare and hard to copy, and how long before a rival copies it anyway?
- Is that advantage a competence trap (excellent at something this industry does not reward)?
- Which activity in our value chain would have to change first?
- What would a competitor have to spend to match us there?

## Strategy choice (generic strategies)

- If customers could have only one reason to buy from us, what is it?
- What price premium (or discount) does that position imply? Can our cost structure carry it?
- Who exactly is the customer? Who is deliberately not?
- Which of our current activities does not fit this strategy? Are we willing to stop it?
- What would "stuck in the middle" look like for us, and how will we avoid it?

## Testing (fit and risk)

- What is the strongest rival's most likely response? What does their balance sheet let them do?
- Which assumption, if wrong, breaks this strategy? How could we test it cheaply first?
- What is the smallest first step that would tell us whether this is working?
- Does this strategy ease or worsen our binding growth constraint?

## GLO-BUS specific

- Look at the price vs P/Q map for drones: which corner is empty, and which corner is crowded?
- Our cost per unit is [above / below] the industry average. Which strategies does that make easier?
- Our image rating is [x]. Which strategy depends most on image, and which least?
- Should cameras and drones follow the same strategy? What would make them differ?
- Which region is least contested? What would it take to lead there?
- What will you hold steady for the next three years, so the strategy has time to work?
